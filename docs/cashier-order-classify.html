How the cashier splits delivery orders

Think of the shop as the middle of a compass. When enough drinks are ready, the system looks at each customer on the map, draws one cut through the shop, and makes two rider runs. Those runs are named from the compass: North, North-East, East, South-East, South, South-West, West, or North-West. Section 4 is the exact server algorithm. The rest of the page has a picture for every pair.

1. The everyday picture

A cashier does not type “North” or “South”. They tap Classify orders. The computer counts cups, looks at map pins, and suggests rider runs.

Under 10 cups → one mixed list 10 cups or more → split by direction One rider bag holds 20 cups (default)

Cups = how many drinks are on the order, not how many customers.

4. The algorithm the server follows

The cashier never types a compass name. After they tap Classify, the server groups READY orders with a pin, then the app shows run cards. Route order is calculated after grouping, from the shop.

  1. Choose Manual or Directional Count cups on located orders. Under 10 cups → one cluster labelled All deliveries (manual) No compass names. At 10 cups or more → continue to the cut.
  2. Cut the map into two opposite halves The shop pin is the centre. Each house gets a bearing (0° North, 90° East, 180° South, 270° West). The server tries each house’s bearing as a cut through the shop and keeps the cut that most evenly splits how many orders into two 180° halves (not equal cups). Houses above the shop are not always North — today’s pins choose the cut.
  3. Name each half from average direction The average bearing of that half (circular mean) is mapped to the nearest of eight labels: North, North-East, East, South-East, South, South-West, West, North-West. You never get all eight at once — at most two halves. An empty half is not shown. If houses sit in a corner of a half, names may not look perfectly opposite (for example North with South-East). The cut is still two semicircles.
  4. Pack each half into rider bags Default 20 cups per rider (the shop can change this). Each half is packed separately, oldest first: if the next order would go over the cap, it starts a new run. An order is never split. A 25-cup house is one oversized run. Overflow names look like North run 1 North run 2
  5. Preview the road, then create the job Stops in a run are ordered from the shop. The cashier previews the path and creates the job so a rider can claim it. They can ignore auto-classify and tick orders by hand, as long as they stay under the cup limit.

5. Compass diagram — direction from the shop

The shop pin is the center. For every customer, the system asks: “If I stand at the shop and look at this house, which way am I facing?”

0° is North, 90° East, 180° South, 270° West. Names use these eight points.

6. The cut-line diagram — how North vs South is chosen

It is not “every house above the shop is always North”. The system tries a cut through the shop and picks the cut that most evenly splits the number of orders into two opposite halves (each half is 180°, like slicing a pizza in two).

Then each half gets a name from the average direction of its orders. If today’s houses sit east and west of the shop, the runs are called East and West instead.

7. Cup packing diagram — one rider bag

After the two halves exist, each half is packed oldest-first into bags of 20 cups (or whatever max-cups setting is). An order is never cut in half. If one order is 25 cups, that order is its own run.

8. Scenarios — tap one and look at the map

Classify always makes two opposite halves. The name of each half is whichever of the eight compass labels matches that half’s average direction.

Opposite pairs that can appear on a busy day: North↔South · East↔West · North-East↔South-West · North-West↔South-East.

F. What cannot happen

This ideaReality
Eight named runs at the same time (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW) No. Only two opposite halves, then cup bags inside each half.
Half of one customer’s drinks on North, half on South No. An order stays whole.
Street names or city names as the run title No. Titles come from the compass (and “run 1”, “run 2” if needed).
Guessing a house with no map pin No. It stays unlocated until someone fixes the address.
Always calling the top of the map North, even if all houses are east No. The cut follows today’s pins. Names follow the average direction of each half.
